# SC-Analytics methodology

SC-Analytics starts with the decision, not the tool.

A technically sophisticated system is only useful when it addresses the real operating problem, works with the available data and fits the process in which the decision is made.

Our working sequence is:

1. Understand the business process and the decision that needs to improve.
2. Define the objective, constraints and success criteria.
3. Assess the available data, systems and operational limitations.
4. Establish a baseline before introducing unnecessary complexity.
5. Design the analytical or technical approach.
6. Build the smallest system that can validate the approach properly.
7. Integrate it with the systems and people that will use the result.
8. Validate technical performance and business usefulness separately.
9. Deploy with appropriate environments, controls and observability.
10. Monitor results and refine the system as new evidence becomes available.

The principle behind the process is straightforward: understand before building.

## Technology selection

Technology follows the problem.

A tool is selected because it has a clear responsibility inside the architecture, not because it is fashionable or because adding another logo makes the stack appear broader.

For every material technology used in a project, the documentation should make clear:

- why it is present
- what it connects to
- what responsibility it owns
- what information enters and leaves it
- what operational constraints apply
- what permissions or controls are required
- what alternatives were considered when relevant

## Two levels of communication

Every project should be understandable at two levels.

The executive layer explains the business problem, decision, approach and impact.

The technical layer provides enough evidence to inspect the architecture, data flow, implementation choices, environments, integrations, validation and limitations.
